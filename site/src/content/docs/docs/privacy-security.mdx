---
title: Privacy and security model
description: Exactly where your audio, text and API keys go when you use Abe, and what Abe does to keep them safe.
---

Abe's privacy comes from its architecture, not from a promise: **there is no Abe server**. No accounts, no telemetry, no relay in the middle.

## Data flows

| Data | Local mode | Cloud mode |
|---|---|---|
| Microphone audio | Processed in memory on your computer, then discarded | Sent over HTTPS directly to your chosen provider |
| Transcribed text | Inserted at your cursor and saved to your local history | Returned by the provider, inserted at your cursor and saved to your local history |
| Voice Transform | Selected text and your spoken instruction go to your transform provider (cloud, or a local server such as Ollama) | Same, to your chosen transform provider |
| API keys | Not needed | Stored in the OS keychain, sent only to that provider |
| Settings, dictionary, history | Stored locally on your computer | Stored locally on your computer |
| Usage data or telemetry | None | None |

The only requests Abe makes on its own are the **update check** (a small file from GitHub) and, in Local mode, the **one-time model download**.

## Your provider's policies

In Cloud mode your audio is handled by the provider under the terms you accepted when you created your key:

- **OpenAI:** [API data controls](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/your-data). OpenAI says API data isn't used to train models by default and may be retained for up to 30 days for abuse monitoring.
- **Groq:** [Your data in GroqCloud](https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data) and the [Groq privacy policy](https://groq.com/privacy-policy/).
- **Custom endpoints:** whoever operates them. Self-hosting keeps everything on hardware you control.

## API key handling

- Keys are stored in the OS credential store: **Windows Credential Manager** or the **macOS Keychain**.
- Provider requests are made by Abe's native (Rust) backend. The key isn't handed to the app's web interface, and there's no command that reads it back out.
- Keys are never written to logs.

## What Abe can access, and why

Abe needs a few powerful permissions to work as a system-wide tool:

- **Microphone:** used only while you're recording (holding the hotkey, or between toggles).
- **Accessibility / input simulation:** to paste or type text into the app you're using.
- **Automation (macOS):** to paste text into other applications.
- **Global hotkeys / Input Monitoring:** to notice your shortcut while another app has focus. Abe reacts to its configured shortcuts only and doesn't record your keystrokes.
- **Clipboard:** paste mode puts your text on the clipboard to insert it.

## Verify it yourself

Abe is [open source](https://github.com/HardHeadHackerHead/honest-abe). You can audit every network request, build it yourself, or watch its traffic with a tool like Wireshark or Little Snitch.
